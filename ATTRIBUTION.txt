files made from it. Keep this file with the data when you redistribute it.

Earth regional geology rules v1 (2026-09-29, frozen for existing saves) and v2 (2026-10-02, new worlds)

GLiM: Hartmann, Jens; Moosdorf, Nils (2012): Global Lithological Map Database v1.0 (gridded to 0.5 degree spatial resolution). PANGAEA. https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.788537
Supplement: Hartmann & Moosdorf (2012), The new global lithological map database GLiM, https://doi.org/10.1029/2012GC004370

GUM: Boerker, Janine; Hartmann, Jens; Amann, Thorben; Romero-Mujalli, Gibran (2018): Global Unconsolidated Sediments Map Database v1.0 (shapefile and gridded to 0.5 degree spatial resolution). PANGAEA. https://doi.org/10.1594/PANGAEA.884822
Supplement: Boerker et al. (2018), Terrestrial Sediments of the Earth, https://doi.org/10.1002/2017GC007273

Both datasets licensed Creative Commons Attribution 3.0 Unported: https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/3.0/
Changes: categorical ASCII grids rebaked as unsigned-byte Base64 arrays; nodata=0. GUM regridded by containing source cell at GLiM cell centres, without numeric interpolation or extrapolation. The same grid file serves both rules versions. Rules v2 additionally, at load time and without editing the file: fills GLiM ocean/water/no-data/ice cells within 2 cells of mapped land with the nearest mapped cell's categories (so coastal land and islands that fall in ocean cells get bedrock), and assigns each point to the nearest of one jittered seed per cell (irregular contacts instead of the source grid squares). Runtime selects source categories; game-scale stratigraphy, material proxies, hydrology conditions, the broad Baltic pilot envelope and the v2 chalk envelopes (southern England, Paris Basin, Denmark/Scania/Rugen, Austin Chalk, Selma/Black Belt) are Earth-authored approximations drawn from general published geological knowledge, not digitised survey contacts, outputs of the source authors, or endorsements by the source authors or the survey organisations named in the rules file.

GUM has no source coverage north of 83.514743N. GLiM coarse water/ice/no-data are not evidence of absent bedrock. Unknown bedrock preserves legacy strata. Metallic ore geography is handled by the separate regional ore rules; vanilla bauxite strata (and in v2 kimberlite and suevite, the diamond hosts) are never replaced by the regional strata.

Source: Global Lakes and Wetlands Database, version 2 (GLWD v2), combined classes grids (GeoTIFF, 15 arc-seconds).

https://doi.org/10.5194/essd-17-2277-2025
Data: https://www.hydrosheds.org/products/glwd (reference information: https://doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.28519994)
(The GLWD v2.0 Technical Documentation of April 2025 also lists Matthews, E. among the authors of this citation.)

Licence: Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/
The GLWD authors ask that the data are not redistributed whole in their original format; Earth ships only the
aggregate described below, not the GLWD grids. The data come "as is" and the GLWD authors do not endorse Earth.

Changes made: the per-pixel dominant wetland class (GLWD_v2_0_main_class) and total wetland percentage
(GLWD_v2_0_area_pct) were combined, each pixel's whole wetland percentage counting for its dominant class, and
aggregated to the share of each 0.1 degree cell (180 W to 180 E, 84 N to 60 S; sea counts as dry) covered by seven
groups of GLWD classes, stored as bytes (share x 250):
  bog          open peatland: 23, 25, 27 (arctic/boreal, temperate, tropical/subtropical peatland, non-forested)
  peatforest   wooded peatland: 22, 24, 26
  swampforest  flooded swamp forest: 8, 10, 12, 16 (lacustrine, riverine regularly or seasonally flooded, palustrine
               regularly flooded; forested)
  marsh        flooded open marsh and delta: 9, 11, 13, 17 (non-forested), 30 large river delta, 31 other coastal wetland
  mangrove     28
  saltmarsh    29
  damp         seasonally saturated or ephemeral ground: 14, 15, 18, 19, 20, 21, forested or not (GLWD's least certain
               classes; their wooded or open state is largely today's land use, so Earth reads them as wet ground only)
Lakes, reservoirs, rivers and streams (1-7), salt pans (32) and rice paddies (33) are left out. Minor classes inside
mixed 15" pixels are not represented. GLWD v2 maps contemporary conditions; Earth's use of it (for any period) is an
approximation by Earth, not by the GLWD authors.

Floodplains (floodplains.bin)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Earth floodplain grid (config/floodplains.bin, baked 2026-11-08 by tools/FloodplainBake; decision 0048, soil rules 3)

What it is: the level (m) the natural flood of Earth's dry-season rivers reaches, on a 15 arc-second grid, for arid land
only. In worlds with ground columns and soil rules 3, arid ground at or below that level keeps its topsoil (the Nile
valley and delta, Mesopotamia, the Indus plain). It is Earth's own model of a flood, not a measured flood map.

Sources:
- Copernicus DEM GLO-90 (the Copernicus WorldDEM(TM)-90, COP-DEM_GLO-90-DGED/2022_1), as Earth's GLO-90 worlds keep it
  (VintagestoryData/ModData/earth/glo90, with the reservoirs of its dam list removed). Used under the "Licence for
  Copernicus DEM instance COP-DEM-GLO-90-F Global 90m Full, Free & Open".
  Produced using Copernicus WorldDEM(TM)-90 © DLR e.V. 2010-2014 and © Airbus Defence and Space GmbH 2014-2018 provided
  under COPERNICUS by the European Union and ESA; all rights reserved. The organisations in charge of the Copernicus
  programme by law or by delegation do not incur any liability for any use of the Copernicus WorldDEM(TM)-90.
- Earth's rivers: HydroRIVERS v1.0 (Lehner, B., Grill G. (2013): Global river hydrography and network routing: baseline
  data and new approaches to study the world's large river systems. Hydrological Processes, 27(15): 2171-2186; data at
  www.hydrosheds.org, used under the HydroSHEDS license) as laid onto GLO-90 by Earth (rivers-glo90.bin), with their
  discharge and the share of it from moist catchments.
- Earth's climate (the aridity indices, Holdridge's from the temperatures and rain and the Penman-Monteith one of
  aridity.bin from CHELSA V2.1 BIOCLIM+, that pick which rivers run through dry land), as in Earth's ATTRIBUTION.txt.
Earth is not endorsed by the Copernicus programme, ESA, DLR, Airbus or the HydroSHEDS authors.

How it was made (tools/FloodplainBake, from the terrain model a new 1:10 GLO-90 world builds with earth-1.0.3-tall.22
and its data packs): every river course of 10 m3/s or more, with at least 10 m3/s from moist catchments, where it runs
through land with an aridity index below 0.3 (either index), floods 1.2 x Q^0.3 m (1.5 to 15 m) above its water (its course level or
GLO-90's lowest point within 3", whichever is higher). The flood falls 0.15 m per km of the way out and covers connected
land at or below it, on a 7.5" grid whose ground is the lowest GLO-90 point within about 6" (under town and palm
heights); seas and lakes stop it; it never stands above the top of the river nearest in a straight line, nor covers
ground more than one flood height below that river's water. Each 15" cell keeps the highest flood among its four 7.5"
cells, the cells around the flood the same level less one cell's fall; levels are stored in decimetres.
